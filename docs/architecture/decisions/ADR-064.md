### ADR-064 — [2026-09-04] — GRAM readback is the sanctioned render-verification mechanism, on demand only

**Status**: accepted
**As-built**: 2026-09-09 (TASK-638, `983cd54`). `get sig` (D1/D3), `set now`/`get now` (D5), `T_DH_05` (D4), `T_CLK_SIG_01` (D3/D7/D8) — all DUT-verified; `.dram0.bss` +8 B measured on the map, headroom 7 976 B.
**Ruled**: human, 2026-09-04, on the Architect's decision B
([M-HARNESS2-architect-review.md](../designs/M-HARNESS2-architect-review.md) §9). The human's words
were **"on demand only"**, and that phrase is the binding constraint of this ADR, not a preference —
see D2.
**Design doc**: [M-HARNESS2 architect review](../designs/M-HARNESS2-architect-review.md) §3
**Requirement**: [R5](../../verification/M-HARNESS2-requirements.md), finding `H-2` (a clock face
rendering as a solid black rectangle passes all fourteen ids and all five milestone criteria)

---

**Context**

R5 asks for "a device-computed signature over the buffer the app drew from". **On this target there is
usually no such buffer.** A repo-wide grep finds `TFT_eSprite` in exactly two places —
`app/src/aquarium/aquariumApp.h:226` and `app/src/stock/stockHeatmap.cpp:182`. Everything else,
including the Clock family this requirement exists for, draws immediate-mode to the global `tft`.
Clock's own source explains why: `app/src/apps/clockApp.h:194` records that a full-tube `uint16_t`
scratch is 10.6 KB, "enough to overflow this board's tight DRAM budget", so the Nixie renderer tints
in bands. A shadow framebuffer is not available either — 320×240×2 is 150 KB against a 290 KB
INTERNAL ceiling with 60 KB reserved for TLS (`app/mem_manifest.yaml`).

Hooking the render path is worse than unavailable: TFT_eSPI's draw entry points are not virtual in
the general case, the library is a `lib_deps` dependency rather than a vendored fork (unlike `SD` and
`WiFiClientSecure` in `app/lib/`), and a tee over draw primitives would hash **intent, not result** —
reporting a face as drawn even when the SPI write never reached the panel, which is one of the
failure modes worth catching.

The mechanism R5 actually needs is already shipped and DUT-proven.
`app/src/debug/serialConsole/cmdMisc.cpp:108` calls `tft.readRect()` in 8-row bands inside
`screendump`; MISO is wired (`app/platformio.ini:37`) and the read clock was *empirically* lowered to
2.5 MHz for signal integrity by TASK-340 (`app/platformio.ini:57`). `cmdColorProbe`
(`app/src/debug/serialConsole/cmdMisc.cpp:149`) verified the whole write→read round trip against
known RGB565 values **25/25**.

**Decision**

**D1 — the sanctioned mechanism is a device-computed signature over `tft.readRect()`, exposed as a
console command.** `get sig <x> <y> <w> <h>` returns, in one JSON line, `{hash, inkCount,
distinctColors, bgColor}` — `screendump`'s existing band loop
(`app/src/debug/serialConsole/cmdMisc.cpp:99-108`) with the base64 encode and the `Serial.write`
replaced by a rolling FNV-1a and three counters. It reuses `screendump`'s per-call `malloc`/`free`
band buffer (`app/src/debug/serialConsole/cmdMisc.cpp:87`), so it costs **0 B of static RAM** and a few hundred bytes of debug flash, and it works identically for all thirteen apps, sprite
or not, with **no per-app code at all**.

**D2 — on demand only. This is a constraint, not a default.** The signature is computed **only when a
test asks for it**, from the host, between frames. Concretely, and each of these is a **violation of
this decision**, not a future option:

* a call to the signature routine from any render or `tick()` path — including a "cheap" incremental
  hash maintained as the app draws;
* a timer, ticker, task or periodic dispatch that computes a signature on its own schedule;
* a harness-side polling loop that calls `get sig` on a fixed interval rather than at named points in
  a test body. **The constraint binds the caller as well as the firmware** — a 10 Hz host poll is a
  timer wearing a test's clothes, and this is a faithful reading of "never on a timer", not a
  widening of it.

Any future proposal for a periodic or in-render signature **supersedes this ADR** and must be taken
as a new decision with its own frame-cost measurement. It is not reachable by amendment.

**D3 — ink and entropy metrics ship beside the hash, and they are the load-bearing half.** The single
most valuable assertion needs no golden and no time freeze: `inkCount > N` **and**
`distinctColors > 1` over the canvas turns `H-2` — a solid black rectangle passing fourteen ids —
into a failing constant, today, with one command. `bgColor` is returned so the assertion can name
what it counted against.

**D4 — every signature-based id is preconditioned on a live readback channel, in the HEALTH class.**
A degraded MISO read is TASK-340's exact, already-observed failure, and without a precondition it
reports **every** signature comparison as a firmware defect — the negative-assertion hazard R12
describes, one layer down. A `colorprobe`-shaped write/read round trip becomes a sixth HEALTH check
(amending R39). A HEALTH failure is exit 4 and `NOT-RUN` for everything downstream
([M-TESTARCH precedence](../designs/M-TESTARCH-precedence-hierarchy.md) §4 rule 3), which is the
correct polarity: an unreadable panel means the board is not a valid subject, not that the firmware
is broken.

**D5 — `set now` is a named prerequisite, not an optional extra.** `app/src/debug/serialConsole/cmdSet.cpp`
has no time injection of any kind. Without one, no golden signature over any Clock **digit** region is
writable, and the family R5 exists for would be verified only over regions that do not contain the
thing the family is about. `set now <epoch>` plus a "clock frozen" flag under `SERIAL_DEBUG` is ~8 B
and a branch, and TASK-638 does not close without it.

**D6 — what a signature may and may not be asserted against.**

| Assertion shape | Legitimate? |
|---|---|
| golden hash over a **static chrome** sub-region (tube outlines, bezel, VFD off-segments) | **yes** — RGB565, no dithering, no alpha, no antialiasing in the paths this project uses, and `colorprobe`'s 25/25 says the round trip is bit-exact |
| golden hash over any region containing the time, a blink phase, an animation frame, RSSI or a VU level | **no** — `app/src/apps/clockApp.h:151` blinks the colon by design; the hash changes every second |
| differential (`sig(styleA) != sig(styleB)`, ≥2 distinct signatures across an animation window) | **yes** |
| structural (`inkCount`, `distinctColors`) | **yes**, and needs neither a golden nor D5 |

**D7 — signature regions are derived from the generated layout, never typed.** The panel is 320×240
with a 45 px taskbar over a 275×240 canvas; `shell_layout.h` is generated and `run/audit-origin`
exists for exactly this class of error. Typed coordinates would make the project's newest mechanism
the one place its own mirror rule (R42) is unapplied.

**D8 — the two operational constraints of the shipped command carry over and must be restated in the
test contract.** The TWDT is 15 s with `panic=true`, so any multi-band loop feeds it
(`app/src/debug/serialConsole/cmdMisc.cpp:106`); and the app must be quiescent when the region is
read — `get idle` (TASK-518) is
the predicate. A signature taken mid-repaint is a torn read, and a torn read is a false FAIL.

**Alternatives rejected**

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| Hash "the buffer the app drew from", as R5 is written | No such buffer exists for eleven of thirteen apps, including the whole Clock family. |
| Add a shadow framebuffer to create one | 150 KB against a 290 KB INTERNAL ceiling with 60 KB reserved for TLS. Not on the table. |
| Tee the TFT_eSPI draw calls | Entry points are not virtual, the library is not vendored, and it would hash intent rather than result — passing a face whose SPI write never reached the panel. |
| `run/screendump` + host-side image comparison per visual claim | One ~18 s serial dump per assertion; at Clock's fourteen ids that is ~4 minutes of a 60-minute DUT budget for one family. This is the fallback if D1 ever stops working, not the design. |
| A periodic device-side signature published as a heartbeat field | Refused by D2. It puts an SPI panel read in the frame budget of a board whose read clock had to be dropped 8× for signal integrity, to save the harness one round trip. |

**Consequences**

- *Easier*: every visual claim in the suite becomes falsifiable through one command, with no per-app
  firmware. `H-2`'s black rectangle fails on the first assertion written.
- *Harder*: the suite acquires a hardware dependency it did not have — a board revision that unwires
  MISO, or a panel whose GRAM does not read back, removes the mechanism for every visual id at once.
  D4 makes that arrive as a HEALTH failure rather than as a wave of firmware FAILs, which is the most
  this ADR can do about it.
- *Constrained*: read cost is arithmetic — `readRect` moves 3 bytes/pixel at 2.5 MHz, so ~69 ms for a
  120×60 clock face and ~0.74 s for the full panel. Full-panel signatures inside a test body are
  affordable once, not per-frame; D2 is what keeps that true.
- *Cost*: 0 B static RAM; a few hundred bytes of debug flash for `get sig`, ~8 B and a branch for
  `set now`. The Clock family's existing ids are **rewritten, not migrated** (TASK-639) — five
  M-CLOCK-STYLES exit criteria are re-recorded DEFERRED under TASK-587 rather than silently
  re-scored.
